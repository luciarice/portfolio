Provocation and Confrontation: Audience Perception and Experience of Self-Injury in the Context of Performance Art.

Examining the use of self-injury within performance art, dissecting how audience experience, interaction, and perceptions are affected. With a focus on the works of Marina Abramović and Gina Pane, the intention and reasoning for utilising self-injury within their performances are explored, finding that reasons of cultural and political commentary are a common thread throughout the performances discussed. Using David Humes theory of experience and the arguments brought forward by Amelia Jones in Presence in Absentia: Experiencing Performance as Documentation, I argue that through live audiences, performance art creates a heightened audience experience through use of sensory amplification, shock, the unknown, and the opportunity for interaction, and by witnessing performance without prior knowledge, audience experience is heightened and intensified. As performance art makes use of the heightened experience and live audience, it lends itself well as a medium for political commentary and activism, as it utilises direct and provocative imagery in a way which incites strong audience reaction. Lastly pointing out that artists push their works and audiences to extremes, resulting in consequences of a legal nature. Leading to censorship and arrest, this affects both audience experience and perception of the artists and their works.

The Art of The Artificial.

This collectio aims to highlight the artistry which coincides with functional everyday items, highlighting the delicate and intriging designs of personal accessories crafted from arteficial materials. The decorative designs of functional accessories from glasses to combs, to buckles and to fans, are showcased to celebrate the merging of fashion and function.

As synthetic materials began to come into popularity in the mid to late 1800s, by the early 20th century plastics had fully taken off and found their way into the everyday item. More and more synthetics were being used and their diverse materiality and uses being utilised, as well as a number of manufacturing processes such as injection moulding and hand cutting. Intricate forms, colourful patterns and functional designs are all popping up more and more, showcased by this collections.

How can function not only exist alongside fashion but also be enhanced by it? Exploring how the artistry of an item can be an addition to its function, if the goal of an item is to accessorise.

This exhibition is made up from a series of items from the Museum of Design in Plastics, each belonging to their collection and some specifically ro the Plastics Historical Society (PHS) Collection.

With Reference to the Interpretation of Monet's 'Impression Sunrise', How Does a Focus on Matters of Place and Space Help to Reframe the More Familiarly Asked Historical Questions of "How", "When", and "Why".

When analysing art there are a series of questions one must ask to form an understanding of a piece. These questions are "how", "when", and "why". However, this essay argues that the question of "where" not only is of equal importance but ca majorly affect how we think about the other vital questions. "where" can be interperated in a number of ways, such as where was the piece created, where did it exhibit, where does it depict, aswell as questions about the artists, where were they from, where did their gain inspiration, where did they live, etc. These questions are imperative to iunderstanding artworks and form the context and base of knowledge from which we can further interpret. Arther Danto, art critic and philosopher considers this point, 'The possible inyterpretations are constrained by the artist's location in the world, by when and where they lived, by what experiences [they] could have had".

The Art of Losing Your Mind: How Female Suffering is Depicted in Contemporary Art and Media.

The idea of sadness, of pain and of suffering is a gendered concept. In his essay Mourning and Melancholia, Freud wrote that mourning is an inherently feminine action whereas melancholy is an inherently masculine action (Freud, 1917, pp. 243-58). In her book Artemisia Gentileschi: The Shaping and Reshaping of an Artistic Identity, Art historian and writer, Mary D. Garrard stated that

‘‘When the male melancholic appropriates the feminine, loss is tragic, despair is heroic, and emptiness is pregnant with creative possibility. Cast as a female, the melancholic risks appearing merely pathetic.’ (Garrard, 2001, pp.72)


This dissertation aims to look at how female suffering is depicted within contemporary art and media whilst discussing the difference between how male and female artists depict the same theme. I will do this by first looking at how female suffering is romanticised with specific reference to literature, then looking at how female suffering is depicted by male artists, how they enjoy depicting women in pain, with reference to their personal lives and how their personal relationships manifest within their work. Finally I will be looking at pain as a protest, when art about female pain is created by female artists, how does the autobiographical nature of this work translate to politically motivated protest. The aim of this research is to identify the disconnect between male and female artists and their varying depictions of female pain, exploring how women depict pain as a protest against the sexualisation from men and their depictions of female pain. Exploring how these two groups not only differ but oppose each other.

When looking at female pain within art, first, we must consider if the goal of the piece was to depict a woman suffering or merely to just depict suffering, with the figure just happening to be a woman. This is where the distinction between male and female artists becomes relevant, as when a female artist depicts female pain and suffering, it is easy to come to the conclusion that it is a reflection of either her own pain or the pain of the women around them, however, when a male artist depicts female pain it may be assumed that the artist has made up a woman and given her pain purely because he likes to see women suffer; that when women depict female pain it is feminist and when men depict female pain it is misogynistic.

The Rise and Prevalence of Nylon for Hosiery in North America Within the Context of the Japanese Silk Boycotts of the 1930s and 40s.

Nylon, from the moment it his fashion had found signioficant sucess and was a crusial breakthrough within the textiles industry. Its rise in popularity can be atributed to its numerous benefits as a fabric such as its durability as its silk-like feel. This essay however examines Nylons success through the context of the Japanese silk boycotts which took place immediately preceding Nylon's launch and during its first few years on the market. When examining the rise and prevelance of Nylon within North America, the political landscape surrounding the introduction of Nylon and its early years must be considered, as I belive they play a critical role in its success.
